### Execution & analysis
- **Much better distribution than the Jun 24 tune-up.** Jun 24 was 75.6 / 77.1 / **80.0** / 58.7 — a progressive fade with lap 3 collapsing. Here it was 73.4 / 78.5 / **76.6** / 57.9 — lap 3 was *faster* than lap 2, and the last 400 m held together. **The lap-3 fade that defined the whole season did not happen.**
- The opening 400 (73.4) was quicker than the plan (76–77) — the standing-start 100 (16.0 s / 63.9 pace) pulled it down. But unlike Jun 24, going out fast did **not** cost the back half: laps 2–3 stabilised at 76–78 and the kick was there (57.9 last 300). Either the fitness carried it or the calf changed the effort distribution — but the result speaks for itself.
- **HR maxed 160** — believable for a maximal 1500 (unlike Jun 24's faulty ≤149). Sat 144 avg / 156–160 in the back half. Genuine hard effort.
- **4:47.64 off a tweaked calf, run in pain, is the standout.** That's a season best on a compromised day, ~4 s under the 2025 PB and inside the ~4:45–4:51 range projected from the June 800 (2:18.31 → ~4:45 personal-exponent projection). Goal for the season was sub-4:45; missed the number but beat the PB and executed the race far better.
- **New On spikes debuted** — plausibly worth a small amount (stiffer plate, better energy return than flats). First race in them; no negative surprises.

### Notes
- **Achilles held; the CALF was the day-before problem** — a different tissue from the June achilles flare, though the same chronically vulnerable chain (see INJURIES.md). Racing hard on a tweaked calf carries real risk; the fact it held to a PB is fortunate as much as anything.
